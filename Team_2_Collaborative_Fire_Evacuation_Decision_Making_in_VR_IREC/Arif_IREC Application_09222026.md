## Part 3: Research Design

### What is the purpose of the research? *(Approximately 250-300 words)*
*What question(s) do you hope to answer? Summarize the objectives and significance of the study.*

The purpose of this study is to investigate collaborative decision-making during simulated fire-evacuation emergencies in virtual reality (VR) when critical information is distributed asymmetrically between two team members. Participants will work in pairs. One participant will act as a Control-Room Analyst and will have access to a building map, fire/smoke sensor information, hazard indicators, and exit status. The second participant will act as an On-Site Responder and will navigate inside the virtual environment without a global map or advance knowledge of the hazard locations. The Responder must communicate with the Analyst and use the Analyst's guidance together with local visual observations to identify and follow a safe evacuation route.

The study addresses three research questions: 
1. How effectively can a Control-Room Analyst guide an information-limited On-Site Responder to a safe evacuation route during simulated fire emergencies?
2. How are communication characteristics, including measured delay, jitter, clarification events, and perceived audio quality, related to evacuation time, route efficiency, navigation errors, and route changes across the three fire scenarios?
3. How do the scenarios affect participants' perceived workload and their evaluation of the communication and VR system?

The three scenarios represent different coordination demands: a blocked primary exit requiring an alternative route, a dynamic hazard that forces the team to revise an initially safe plan, and a competing-routes case in which global sensor information and the Responder's local observations must be combined. Objective task measures will be complemented by NASA-TLX workload ratings, a custom communication-quality questionnaire, and the System Usability Scale (SUS). The study is intended to inform the design of collaborative VR emergency simulations and remote guidance interfaces. It is not intended to validate professional firefighting competence or certify participants in emergency-response procedures.

### Describe the data collection methodology in language that will be understood by researchers outside your field. *(Approximately 250-300 words)*
*Briefly describe your data collection method. For example: qualitative (interviews, focus groups, observations, action research), quantitative (surveys, clinical trials, existing data sets, human genetics) and mixed methods). **Copies of all data collection instruments must be attached to this application, in the required languages**.*

The study will use a mixed-methods, repeated-measures design combining quantitative task-performance and communication data with standardized questionnaire scores and brief qualitative feedback.

Data will be collected in a controlled indoor setting using two Meta Quest 2 VR headsets connected through a local network. Participants will complete the study in pairs. One participant will remain in the Control-Room Analyst role and the other in the On-Site Responder role throughout the experimental session. Before the experimental trials, both participants will receive instructions, complete a short VR familiarization session, and perform one practice task. The order of the three fire scenarios will be counterbalanced across pairs where practicable to reduce order, learning, and fatigue effects.

During each scenario, the VR application will automatically record task-performance and navigation data, including evacuation success, task completion time, movement path, route length, wrong turns, blocked or unsafe route attempts, route changes, and relevant interaction events. The On-Site Responder will navigate using continuous controller-based locomotion rather than teleportation. The communication channel between the Analyst and Responder will be recorded. In addition, communication-system transmission/receipt timestamps and related network logs will be collected to characterize communication delay and jitter. Audio recordings may also be used to verify communication timing and count predefined communication events such as clarification requests or repeated instructions. No video or biometric recordings will be collected.

Participants will complete the following questionnaires and feedback instruments:
- A brief background questionnaire, including age range, previous VR experience, and familiarity with VR controls;
- The NASA Task Load Index (NASA-TLX) after each fire scenario to assess perceived workload;
- A custom communication-quality questionnaire after each fire scenario to assess perceived audio clarity, delay, interruptions, difficulty understanding instructions, and overall communication effectiveness;
- The System Usability Scale (SUS) after completion of all three scenarios to assess overall perceived usability of the VR system, followed by brief open-ended comments about communication, navigation, usability, and discomfort.

### Briefly describe the data analysis processes. *(Approximately 150-300 words)*

Data analysis will combine quantitative analysis of task performance, communication measures, and questionnaire scores with a basic qualitative summary of participants' open-ended comments.

For each fire scenario, objective outcomes will include evacuation success, total completion time, navigation time, route efficiency, actual distance traveled, wrong turns, blocked or unsafe route attempts, and route changes. Results will first be summarized using descriptive statistics and then compared across the three repeated scenarios using appropriate within-participant or paired-team statistical methods. Because the collaborative task is performed by two people, pair-level outcomes such as evacuation time will be analysed at the pair level rather than treating the two participants as independent observations.

Communication-system logs will be used to calculate or summarize delay and jitter where technically available. Audio recordings will be used only for the predefined communication analysis, such as verifying timing and counting clarification requests, repetitions, or communication breakdowns; the study will not perform voice identification or linguistic profiling.

NASA-TLX scores will be summarized for each scenario and compared to examine perceived workload. The custom communication questionnaire will be analysed as a subjective measure of perceived communication quality. SUS will be scored after the full session as a measure of overall perceived usability of the VR system.

Exploratory analyses will examine whether poorer communication metrics are associated with longer evacuation times, lower route efficiency, more navigation errors, or higher workload. These associations will be interpreted cautiously and will not be described as causal unless the communication variable is deliberately manipulated in a future approved protocol.

Open-ended comments will be grouped into recurring themes such as navigation difficulty, communication problems, useful interface features, confusing elements, and VR discomfort.

All analyses and reports will use coded data and aggregated results; raw identifiable audio will not be included in presentations or publications.

### 4.8 Procedures in the event of a participant withdrawing from the study
*Outline any follow up actions which will occur if a participant withdraws from the study.*

If a participant chooses to withdraw, they may do so at any time, without giving a reason and without any penalty or negative consequence. The researcher will immediately stop that participant’s involvement in the study and, if the withdrawal occurs during a VR session, the headset will be removed, and the participant will be given an opportunity to rest.

Withdrawal of one participant will also end the current collaborative trial, since the task cannot continue with only one user. The remaining participant will not be required to continue that trial and may, where appropriate, be invited to complete the study at another time with a different partner.

The withdrawing participant will be asked whether they would like any data already collected from their participation to be excluded from the study. If the data are still identifiable through a temporary participant or pair code, the relevant individual and pair-level trial data will be removed. Once data have been fully anonymized and incorporated into aggregated results, it may no longer be possible to identify and remove an individual participant’s data.

### 5.2 Procedures
*Describe how subjects will be involved in detail. Describe the setting in which the participants’ involvement will take place. Where will they be? Will they be alone or in a group? Will there be any specific conditions? How long will it take?*

Participants will take part in paired experimental sessions in the VR laboratory at Nazarbayev University. Both participants will use the collaborative VR system. One participant will act as the **Control-Room Analyst** and will remain primarily stationary with access to a building map, fire/smoke sensor readings, hazard indicators, and exit status. The other participant will act as the **On-Site Responder** and will navigate through the virtual facility using continuous controller-based locomotion. The Responder will not be given a global map or advance knowledge of hazard locations and must communicate with the Analyst to identify a safe evacuation route. A complete session is expected to take approximately 50-60 minutes.

#### 1. Introduction, eligibility confirmation, and consent (5-10 minutes)
On arrival, participants will receive a plain-language explanation of the study, the paired roles, audio recording, expected duration, possible VR discomfort, confidentiality, and the voluntary nature of participation. Eligibility will be confirmed, written informed consent will be obtained, and each participant/pair will receive a study code. A brief background and VR-experience questionnaire will be completed.

#### 2. VR familiarization and practice trial (5-10 minutes)
Participants will receive a tutorial on their assigned roles and VR controls. The Analyst will practice reading the map and sensor interface, while the Responder will practice continuous controller-based locomotion, turning, and following verbal directions. One short practice task will be completed before the experimental scenarios begin.

#### 3. Experimental fire-evacuation scenarios (approximately 20-25 minutes total)
Each pair will complete three non-graphic fire-emergency scenarios. The scenario order will be counterbalanced across pairs where practicable, and the layouts will be designed to provide comparable task lengths while presenting different coordination demands:
- **Scenario 1 - Blocked Primary Exit:** The most direct route to an exit is blocked by a fire/smoke hazard. The Analyst must identify and communicate an alternative safe route to the Responder. This scenario provides a baseline collaborative navigation task.
- **Scenario 2 - Dynamic Hazard Update:** An initially safe route becomes unsafe during the trial when new sensor information indicates a developing hazard. The Analyst must recognize the change, interrupt the previous plan, and redirect the Responder to a different route.
- **Scenario 3 - Competing Routes:** Several routes appear possible, but they differ in safety or accessibility. The Analyst must combine map and sensor information with the Responder's local observations to select and communicate the safest available route.

#### 4. Questionnaires and short breaks between scenarios
After each scenario, both participants will complete NASA-TLX and the custom communication-quality questionnaire. Short breaks will be offered between scenarios, particularly if either participant reports dizziness, nausea, eye strain, fatigue, or other discomfort.

#### 5. System usability and final feedback (approximately 5 minutes)

### 7.1 Is the research Minimal Risk?
> **Minimal risk** means that the probability and magnitude of harm or discomfort anticipated in the research are not greater in and of themselves than those ordinarily encountered in daily life or during the performance of routine physical or psychological examinations or tests.

- **Do you believe those risks will be no greater than minimal?**  
  **Yes** [x] &nbsp;&nbsp; **No** [ ]

  The anticipated risks are no greater than minimal and consist primarily of temporary VR-related discomfort, mild emotional stress from a simulated fire emergency, and a limited confidentiality risk associated with voice recording. - **If risks are greater than minimal, describe the following:**
  - **Explain why these risks are essential to your study:**  
    VR is essential because the study examines collaborative fire-evacuation decision-making in an immersive environment where one participant has global map/sensor information and the second participant must navigate locally and respond to verbal guidance. VR allows controlled presentation of hazards, route changes, and navigation choices without exposing participants to a real fire or other physical emergency.
  - **What have you done to minimize risks without compromising your research objectives?**  
    Risks will be minimized through pre-screening for conditions that may make VR use unsafe, a short practice session, moderate continuous-locomotion speed, snap turning where appropriate, short scenario durations, breaks between scenarios, non-graphic fire/smoke effects, and immediate stopping on request. Participants will be instructed to report nausea, dizziness, eye strain, anxiety, or other discomfort as soon as it occurs.     If a participant experiences significant VR-related discomfort, such as nausea, dizziness, disorientation, or motion sickness, the headset will be removed immediately and the participant will be allowed to sit and rest. The session will not resume unless the participant reports that they feel well and voluntarily wishes to continue. If symptoms persist or become more serious, the session will end and the participant will be advised to seek appropriate medical assistance, including university medical services where appropriate.
  - **What procedures have you established for reporting adverse events should they occur?**  
    Any adverse event occurring during the study will be documented by the research team and reported to the Nazarbayev University IREC in accordance with the university’s adverse-event reporting procedures.
