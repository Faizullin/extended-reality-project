## Introduction

You are invited to take part in a research study about collaborative decision-making in Virtual Reality (VR). Before you decide whether to participate, please read the information below. You may ask any questions you have before signing this form. Participation is entirely voluntary.

---

## Purpose of the Study

The purpose of this study is to investigate collaborative decision-making during simulated fire-evacuation emergencies in Virtual Reality (VR) when important information is distributed between two team members. Two participants will work together: a Control-Room Analyst will have access to a building map, fire/smoke sensor information, hazard indicators, and exit status, while an On-Site Responder will navigate inside the virtual environment without a global map or advance knowledge of hazard locations. The study examines how communication and coordination relate to evacuation performance, route efficiency, navigation errors, workload, and system usability. The study is a research prototype and is not professional firefighting or emergency-response training.

---

## What Will I Be Asked to Do?

You will participate as part of a two-person team. One person will be assigned the role of a Control-Room Analyst and the other will be an On-Site Responder. The Analyst will remain primarily stationary and use a building map, fire/smoke sensor readings, hazard indicators, and exit status. The Responder will use a Meta Quest 2 headset and navigate through the virtual facility without a global map, using local observations together with the Analyst's verbal guidance to identify and follow a safe evacuation route.

- Complete a brief consent and background stage, including age range, previous VR experience, and familiarity with VR controls.
- Receive a short tutorial and complete one practice trial so you are familiar with your assigned role, the VR controls, and the study tasks.
- Complete three non-graphic fire-evacuation scenarios: a blocked primary exit, a dynamic hazard update that requires route replanning, and a competing-routes scenario.
- Communicate with your partner to identify and follow a safe evacuation route. The On-Site Responder will move using continuous controller-based locomotion rather than teleportation.
- After each scenario, complete the NASA-TLX workload questionnaire and a communication-quality questionnaire. After all scenarios, complete the System Usability Scale (SUS) and provide brief open-ended feedback about communication, navigation, usability, and discomfort.

The VR system will automatically record task-performance and navigation information, including evacuation success, completion time, movement path, route length, wrong turns, blocked or unsafe route attempts, route changes, and relevant interaction events. Communication-system timestamps and network logs may also be collected to characterize delay and jitter. Audio communication between you and your partner will be recorded to verify communication timing and count predefined events such as clarification requests, repeated instructions, or communication breakdowns. No video or biometric recordings will be collected.

---

## Risks and Possible Discomfort

This study is considered minimal risk. You may experience mild and temporary effects commonly associated with VR use, such as eye strain, dizziness, cybersickness or motion sickness, fatigue, or discomfort from wearing the headset. Continuous controller-based locomotion may increase motion discomfort for some participants. The fictional, non-graphic fire scenarios may also cause mild temporary stress because of alarms, smoke effects, time pressure, or safety-related decisions. Risks will be reduced through screening, a practice session, moderate movement speed, snap turning where appropriate, short trials, and breaks between scenarios. You may pause or stop the session immediately at any time. If you feel unwell, the headset will be removed and the session will be stopped.

---

## Benefits

You may receive an indirect benefit from experiencing a collaborative VR system and reflecting on communication and decision-making when team members have different information. There is no guarantee of direct personal benefit. The study may contribute to a better understanding of how remote guidance, communication quality, information asymmetry, workload, and usability affect collaborative navigation and decision-making in simulated fire evacuation. The study is not professional fire-safety training and does not certify participants in emergency-response procedures.

## Confidentiality and Privacy

Your identity will be protected as far as reasonably possible. Your name and contact information will be used only for recruitment, scheduling, and consent. Each participant will receive a participant code (for example, P01) and each pair will receive a pair code (for example, Pair01). These codes, rather than names or student IDs, will be used in VR performance logs, communication logs, questionnaires, analysis files, and coded audio filenames.

Electronic research data, including VR logs, communication logs, questionnaire responses, and coded raw audio recordings, will be stored on a password-protected research computer at Nazarbayev University with access limited to the research team. Signed consent forms and any document linking names to study codes will be stored separately in a locked cabinet. Because a voice may be recognizable, raw audio will be treated as confidential identifiable research data. Audio will be used only for the predefined communication analysis; the study will not perform voice recognition, speaker identification, or unrelated analysis of speech content. Results will be reported only in aggregate or de-identified form, and raw audio clips will not be included in presentations, publications, or shared datasets. Research records will be retained for at least three years after dissemination or final reporting and then securely deleted or destroyed. Although reasonable safeguards will be used, complete confidentiality cannot be guaranteed.

## Voluntary Participation and Withdrawal

Participation is strictly voluntary. You may refuse to participate or withdraw at any time without giving a reason and without penalty or negative consequences. Participation or non-participation will not affect your grades, academic standing, employment, university services, or relationship with Nazarbayev University or the researchers.

If you withdraw during a paired VR trial, the current collaborative trial will stop because it requires two participants. You may ask for data already collected from your participation to be excluded while it can still be linked to your temporary participant or pair code; where applicable, the related individual and pair-level trial data will be removed. Once data have been fully de-identified and incorporated into aggregate results, it may no longer be possible to identify and remove an individual record.
