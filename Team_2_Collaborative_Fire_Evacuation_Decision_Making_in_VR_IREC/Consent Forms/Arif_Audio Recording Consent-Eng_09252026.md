## Purpose of the Audio Recording

During the three collaborative VR fire-evacuation scenarios, the verbal communication between the Control-Room Analyst and the On-Site Responder will be recorded. The audio will be used only for the communication analysis described in the study.

---

## What Will Be Recorded and How It Will Be Used

The recording will contain spoken communication between you and your partner during the experimental scenarios. Researchers may use it to verify communication timing and count predefined events such as clarification requests, repeated instructions, and communication breakdowns. Communication timestamps and network logs may also be used to examine delay and jitter. The study will not use the recording for voice recognition, speaker identification, linguistic profiling, or unrelated analysis. No video or biometric recordings will be collected.

---

## Confidentiality and Storage

Audio files will use participant and/or pair codes rather than names. Because voices may be recognizable, raw audio will be treated as confidential identifiable research data and accessed only by the research team. Files will be stored on a password-protected research computer at Nazarbayev University, separately from signed consent forms and identity-code information. Raw audio clips will not be included in presentations, publications, or shared datasets. Research records will be retained for at least three years after dissemination or final reporting and then securely deleted or destroyed.

---

## Voluntary Consent and Withdrawal

Participation is voluntary. You may withdraw at any time without penalty or negative consequences. While your recording can still be linked to your temporary participant or pair code, you may ask for your collected data to be excluded. Once data have been fully de-identified and incorporated into aggregate results, individual removal may no longer be possible. Audio recording is part of the study procedure; if you do not agree to recording, you should not take part in the recorded study session.
